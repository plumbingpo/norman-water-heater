No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Norman Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a complete diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and what it costs before you say yes.

A lot of water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, a rumbling tank, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is nearly always less expensive and less stressful than an emergency swap after the tank has already leaked. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

A maintenance visit from Norman Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and confirms a safe water temperature. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the heater stays running clean and efficient.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water often need a visit every six months. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and the mineral content of your water, which controls how quickly sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. A flushed tank transfers heat with less effort, a fresh anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Sized for Your Household

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Norman Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than tank size. We'll compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, while a well-sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes around Norman run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Norman Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before they spread. We also verify the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
