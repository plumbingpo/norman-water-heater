A water heater has two slow enemies: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they steal efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Neither one is visible from the outside, and by the time a homeowner notices, the damage is usually done. That is the reason maintenance matters for Norman, OK homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

We work through a full checklist every time. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is replaced when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. Gas heaters get a look at the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust

Should You DIY or Call a Pro?

A few tasks, like checking the temperature setting or lifting the relief valve lever to test it, are fine for homeowners, but most water heater work involves gas lines, venting, 240-volt circuits, or a tank full of scalding water, and that belongs with a professional.
