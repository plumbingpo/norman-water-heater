A tankless water heater does not store hot water at all. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Norman, OK, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Error codes are a starting point, not a verdict, and we treat them that way on every Norman call. The same ignition failure code can come from a worn igniter, a fouled flame rod, a gas line too small to feed the burner at full fire, or an air intake clogged with lint. That is why Norman Water Heater tests before replacing anything. Our technicians read the fault history stored in the control board, watch flow sensor output with a faucet open, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, and put a manometer on the gas inlet to see what pressure actually reaches the valve when the burner is running flat out. We also compare inlet and outlet temperatures against the flow rate, which exposes a scaled heat exchanger long before it fails outright.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Start with what is at stake. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Scale is removed with a pump through the service valves rather than vinegar poured in and left to chance. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
