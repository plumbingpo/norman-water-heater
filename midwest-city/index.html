Keeping Your Midwest City Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement.
